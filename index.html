What is it

What is an XRP swap

An XRP swap exchanges XRP for another asset, or the reverse, at a quoted rate. This interface offers two routes: the XRP Ledger's built-in decentralized exchange for on-ledger swaps, and cross-chain routes for assets that live on other networks such as BTC and ETH.

On the XRP Ledger

The XRPL has a native DEX with order books, path payments and AMM pools. Swapping XRP for an issued token such as a stablecoin needs a trust line to that token's issuer. You sign each transaction from your own XRPL wallet.

Across chains

BTC and ETH are not on the XRP Ledger, so a cross-chain route is used instead. You send one asset to a deposit address and receive the other at the wallet address you provide.

The interface only builds quotes and requests. It does not hold your keys or your funds.

On-ledger vs cross-chain

On-ledger vs cross-chain swaps

The route decides which assets you can reach, what you sign, and where the funds travel. Pick based on the asset you want to end up with.

Which to use

Use the XRPL DEX when both sides of the trade are on the XRP Ledger. Use a cross-chain route when you want BTC or ETH in a wallet on its own network.

How to swap

How to swap XRP

Choose a route

Pick the asset you hold and the asset you want. The interface shows whether an on-ledger or cross-chain route applies and gives a quote before you commit.

Step by step

  1. Select XRP and the asset you want, then enter an amount.
  2. For an on-ledger swap into an issued token, add the trust line to its issuer if your wallet does not have one yet.
  3. Review the quote, the rate and the fees, then paste your receiving address for the destination asset.
  4. On-ledger: sign the transaction in your XRPL wallet. Cross-chain: send the exact amount to the deposit address shown.
  5. Wait for confirmation. The swapped asset arrives in your own wallet.

If you send XRP to an exchange rather than your own wallet, add the destination tag it asks for, or the deposit may not be credited to you.

Fees & reserve

What an XRP swap costs

Transaction fee

Every XRPL transaction burns a tiny fee paid in XRP. It is a fraction of an XRP and does not change with the size of the trade, so keep a small XRP balance for it.

Account reserve

An XRPL account must hold a base reserve of XRP to exist, and each trust line or open offer adds an owner reserve on top. Reserved XRP cannot be spent, and the amounts are set by the network, so check the current values in your wallet.

Trading cost

On the DEX, cost is the spread or AMM pool fee reflected in the quote. On a cross-chain route, the quote includes the exchange margin and the network fee of the asset you receive. Check the output amount before you send.

Security

Staying safe

Non-custodial by design

You use your own wallet and approve every transaction yourself. The interface routes and quotes. It never holds your funds or your private keys, and you should never enter a seed phrase into it.

Before you send

  • Verify the issuer address of any token before adding a trust line. Lookalike issued tokens exist.
  • Copy the deposit address and the destination tag, if one is required, exactly as shown.
  • Check your receiving address on the destination chain twice. Blockchain transfers cannot be reversed.
  • Send only the asset and amount named in the quote to a cross-chain deposit address.
  • Rates can move between quote and settlement, so risk is never zero.
FAQ

XRP Swap FAQ

What is an XRP swap?

An XRP swap is a trade of XRP for another asset such as BTC, ETH or USDT, or the reverse. It can run on the XRP Ledger's own DEX or through a cross-chain route, depending on which asset you want.

Can I swap XRP to BTC or ETH on the XRP Ledger?

No. BTC and ETH do not exist on the XRPL, so that swap uses a cross-chain route. You send XRP to a deposit address and receive BTC or ETH at your own wallet on its network.

Why does my XRP balance not all show as spendable?

An XRPL account must keep a base reserve of XRP to stay active, plus an owner reserve for each trust line or open offer. That reserved XRP is locked while those objects exist, and the amounts are set by the network and can change.

What is a destination tag and when do I need one?

A destination tag is a number attached to an XRP payment that tells a shared account which customer it is for. Many exchanges require it on deposits, and leaving it out can delay or lose the credit. Your own wallet usually does not need one.

What is a trust line on the XRP Ledger?

A trust line is your account's opt-in to hold a token from a specific issuer, such as a stablecoin. You need one before you can receive that token, and it adds an owner reserve in XRP while it is open.

How much does an XRP swap cost in fees?

An on-ledger swap pays a tiny XRP transaction fee plus the spread or pool fee built into the quote. A cross-chain swap adds a rate margin and the network fee of the asset you receive, all shown in the quote before you confirm.
